
## **Chapter 8: My Manifesto of Being Happy**

For a long time, I thought happiness was something that happened *after* I got things done, fixed things, solved things, achieved things, and figured things out.  
Now I see it very differently.

My main intention in life is simply this:

**To BE HAPPY.**

And by happiness I don’t mean a bubbly mood or constant cheerfulness.  
I mean a **state of being** that holds everything I care about most.

When I am being happy, I am content.  
I am loving and caring.  
I feel a sense of enough — I am giving enough and receiving enough.  
I savor my life instead of rushing through it.  
I am present.  
I am peaceful.  
I am connected.  
I feel loved.  
I carry a sense of humor and perspective.  
I am aware of goodness and hope.  
I know that God loves me.  
And I remember that I am human.

This is what happiness feels like in my body and in my mind.  
It is the climate of my life.

### **Being Happy Is Not What I Do — It’s How I Am**

What I have discovered is that happiness is not a task.  
It is not something I *accomplish*.  
It is a way of **being**.

When I am in this state of being, I am living from presence.  
I am aware of this moment as it is.  
I am experiencing life — and also observing it.  
I have perspective.  
I am inside the moment and gently standing beside myself at the same time.

This state of being doesn’t require perfect circumstances.  
It doesn’t wait for problems to disappear.  
It doesn’t demand that life behave.

It simply invites me to come home to myself.

### **What Pulls Me Away from Happiness**

Of course, I am human.  
And there are many things that disturb my happiness.

Lack of sleep.  
Pain.  
Disappointment.  
Fear.  
Worry.  
Seriousness. Lack of a sense of humor   
Problems.  
A sense of doom.  
Disapproval.  
Correction.  
Criticism.  
Unwanted advice.

These experiences don’t mean I am failing at life.  
They mean I am alive.

When I get pulled away from my natural happiness, I don’t scold myself.  
I don’t try to “fix” my feelings.  
I simply recognize that something is asking for care.

### **How I Find My Way Back**

When life intrudes and my state of being shifts, I pause and listen.

What am I feeling?  
What do I need?  
What do I want?  
Do I need to let something go?  
Do I need to do something?  
Do I need to ask for help?  
Do I need to be understood — or help someone else feel understood?

Sometimes I return to happiness by resting.  
Sometimes by crying.  
Sometimes by accepting what is happening and walking through it with grace.  
Sometimes by letting go of the story that is tightening my heart.

Returning to happiness is not about controlling life.  
It is about **remembering who I am.**

### **Holding My Intention**

My primary intention remains steady:

**BE HAPPY.**

Along the way, I may temporarily shift to other intentions:  
hold it together,  
stay calm,  
don’t yell,  
don’t spiral,  
accept what is,  
get through this moment.

These are not replacements for happiness.  
They are **bridges that carry me back to it.**

### **My Daily Practice of Being**

Each day I remind myself of what matters most.

I imagine a big sign in my life that says:

**BE HAPPY.**

I notice when I let things go instead of letting them upset me.  
I notice when I choose happiness over fixing, proving, pushing, or rushing.  
I notice when I am being rather than only doing.

I ask God to help me return to the state I want to live from.  
I read about being.  
I listen to what others say about being.  
I talk with others about what being means to them.  
I keep learning.  
And I keep returning.

### **Why This Choice Changes Everything**

When I live from the state of being happy, my nervous system settles.  
My heart opens.  
My mind clears.  
My relationships soften.  
My body feels safer.  
My life becomes gentler.  
My aging becomes more graceful.  
And my days become a place of belonging.

This is not about perfection.  
This is about orientation.

This is the life I choose.

### **My Promise to Myself**

I choose happiness as my home base.  
I choose being over proving.  
I choose presence over pressure.  
I choose gentleness over harshness.  
I choose to keep returning — again and again — to the beautiful state of **being happy**.

And when I forget, I forgive myself.  
And I come back.

**What state of mind would please you?**

Do we know who we are? What is a view of the various selves that we are?
